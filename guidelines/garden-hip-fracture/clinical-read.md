# Clinical read: garden-hip-fracture@1961.1 (tier 2)

Independent read against S1 (Garden 1961, sha256 a298c248...). Source text checked for the classification sentence (p.654, PDF p.8), the stage-by-stage remarks (pp.655-656) and the summary (p.17). Figs 6-9 (journal pp.651-652, PDF pp.5-6) viewed as page images, not just captions.

## Verdict
Accept with minor edits. No stage definition contradicts the source or the figures. Nothing is imported from outside the paper. Findings below are wording only.

## Stage definitions against captions and figures
- Stage I (Fig 6): matches the caption (greenstick inferior buttress, minimal lateral rotation giving the illusion of impaction, distal medial lamellae abducted relative to the adducted capital fragment, "may at any time become complete").
- Stage II (Fig 7): matches. The image shows a complete line with no visible tilt, consistent with the caption.
- Stage III (Fig 8): matches. The image shows the head tilted (abduction and medial rotation) with the neck still in contact, as the caption says.
- Stage IV (Fig 9): matches. The image shows separated fragments with the head upright and its trabeculae in line with the pelvis, as the caption says.
- Overall the figures and captions agree. The card is faithful to the figures.

## Findings
- Minor, sum.4 and pit.nomgmt: "no prognosis for any stage" is slightly absolute. Garden does tie stage to treatability: Stage IV "form the hard core of subcapital injuries which are particularly resistant to treatment" (p.656), and Stage I "does not arise" for reduction. The card already quotes the Stage IV remark and frames it as operative comment, so this is acceptable. Suggest "no outcome or prognosis data per stage" instead.
- Minor, pit.evidence: "the author calls his findings premature" is said in the context of low-angle fixation results (p.17), not the classification, which he offers "in the hope that it may prove to be of additional value". Reword to say the caveat is about the series.
- Minor, pit.scope: "It is not for extracapsular fractures" is inferred, not stated. The paper only labels the stages "subcapital" and lists extracapsular types in the history paragraph. Reword as "the paper defines the stages only for subcapital fractures".
- Minor, sum.3: "relative to the neck and pelvis" is loose. Fig 6 compares distal and capital trabeculae, Fig 8 uses the head trabeculae, Fig 9 uses alignment with the pelvis. Table row III/IV already carry this correctly.
- Minor, rationale omitted: the paper positions the classification against Pauwels (obliquity after reduction), saying displacement before reduction is the unifying variable. Not needed for a reporting card; optional.
- Minor, possible added pitfall (source-based): Stage IV head can sit in a near-normal position with trabeculae aligned (Fig 9 caption), so alignment alone is not "undisplaced". def.s4 already says so; a pitfall line would make it harder to miss.
- Locators: journal page = PDF page + 646 holds for every locator checked (651/652 = PDF 5/6, 654 = PDF 8, 655-656 = PDF 9-10).
- Outside content: none found. The alignment index values (160/180) and the AVN statements are in the paper (p.654 and p.662).

## For Hassan items
- DECISION (tier 2, no management or prognosis, I/II vs III/IV grouping absent): agree. Garden does not group stages, and the displaced/undisplaced grouping and NICE NG124 pathway are later usage. Keep out of this card. A separate pack from NG124 or an orthopaedic society source is the right route.
- Stage I to IV vs Garden type/grade, aliases carry all: agree. Aliases are search only, and the card itself says the paper uses "Stage".
- Definitions from captions, figures not independently checked: now checked, agree with the captions (see above). This note can be updated to say so.
- status "current" kept: agree. Nothing supersedes it as a description, but the card should keep presenting it as a foundational description, not a validated grading. A reader should not infer inter-observer reliability (the card says there is none reported).
- applies_to finding_type "femoral neck fracture": agree with a caveat. The pitfall carries the subcapital limit. Consider a more specific finding_type such as "subcapital femoral neck fracture" if the matcher allows, so it does not fire on basal or pertrochanteric reports.
