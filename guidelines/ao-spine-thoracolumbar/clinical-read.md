# Clinical read: ao-spine-thoracolumbar@2013.1 (tier 2 reference card)

Independent read against the filed source (Vaccaro AR et al, Spine 2013;38:2028-2037, sources/*.pdf and .txt). Figures 1 and 3-11 (PDF p4-7) viewed as page images and compared with the text and legends. Read-only; nothing in the card was edited.

## Verdict

Clinically faithful. No Critical findings. The card contains no content from outside the source. Every definition (A0-A4, B1-B3, C, N0-N4, NX, M1, M2), tab.morph, tab.neuro, tab.mod, tab.kappa and all pitfalls match the source text, and the figures agree with the definitions. Two Important and several Minor wording points are worth fixing before the card is relied on; none changes a grade.

## Findings

1. Important. pit.nomgmt, sum.4 and For Hassan 5 say the paper gives no treatment for any type or grade. Broadly true, but the source does say that M1 helps decide whether operative stabilisation is considered, that the neurological modifier is "central ... to guide the need for operative intervention", that an ankylosed-spine extension injury is "highly unstable", and that it expects most extension-distraction injuries in ankylosed spines and translational (C) injuries to be treated surgically (p2036). Suggest "gives no treatment rule or score per grade" and carry the authors' expectation for B3/C as an attributed statement, not "no recommendation for any type".
2. Important. status "current" and "Later AO Spine updates ... are not stated" (For Hassan 1): the card cannot support "current" from the filed source. Label it as the 2013 consensus paper, or leave status as the library's convention if that is what the field means. Reports using later modifiers should not be assumed covered or wrong.
3. Minor. def.M1 omits the source's qualifier that M1 is for fractures that "seem stable from a bony standpoint" with indeterminate tension band. This is the case a radiologist most needs; add it so M1 is not applied to a determinate B2.
4. Minor. def.A3 omits "and the spinal canal" (Fig 6 text) and def.A1 omits the legend "vertebral canal is intact". Canal involvement is a useful reading cue and is in the source.
5. Minor. pit.sep says "any vertebral body fracture or tension band injury accompanying a type B or C injury". Per source, for B only the vertebral body fracture is specified separately; for C both body fracture and tension band injury (B1-B3), the latter "if possible". Reword to match.
6. Minor. applies_to.modality is CT and MRI. The source also says accurate classification is possible with CT and/or plain radiographs (p2035). Add radiograph if the vocabulary has it.
7. Minor. tab.kappa: the type A subtype kappas come from single-vertebra type A cases only (Methods, p2029; Table 1 footnote), whereas B and C draw on the whole case series. Worth one line so the table is not read as a like-for-like comparison.

## For Hassan items

1. Library note (2013 paper only): agree. Add the finding 2 caveat about "status".
2. Omitting the n (%) column: agree. It is the raters' estimated case mix, not part of the classification, and could be misread as prevalence.
3. Aliases: agree. "AO type A fracture" and similar are plausible report phrasings; excluding TLICS and Magerl is correct since the source distinguishes them (TLICS has a points score and treatment recommendation; the AOSpine paper does not).
4. region "spine": agree as a stopgap. Thoracolumbar scope is carried by finding_type and pit.region; a matcher keyed on region alone will also match cervical fractures, so this depends on finding_type being used. Hassan's call, but I would accept.
5. No management added: agree, with the finding 1 rewording so the card does not overstate absence.
