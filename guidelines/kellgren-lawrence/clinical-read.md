## Findings

1. Important. applies_to lists "foot" and both spine regions. The paper gives a foot view (AP) only in the views list; foot is not among the eight groups "chosen for detailed study", has no figure and no results. Spine grading covers apophyseal joints only (the card says so in pit.spine). A foot or lumbar report would match this card although the paper offers nothing usable. Narrow, or make the foot/lumbar limits visible where the card is shown.
2. Important. Review claims a lookup pack built from the 1963 Atlas "would stay tier 2" because it has no management. The spec defines tier 1 as executable decision logic, and the existing tier 1 packs (Bosniak, BTA U) do carry recommendations, so the library's own practice (and the Weber ruling) supports it, but say so as library policy, not as a general fact. Also `status: "current"` for a 1957 observer-variation study is generous: the operational KL grades that radiologists use are the Atlas descriptions, which this card cannot supply.
3. Minor. pit.observer says prevalence "varied by about 31 per cent between observers". The paper says deviations of +-31 per cent from the mean value of all readings (observer A vs B1) may be expected; intra-observer was +-5 per cent. Reword to "up to about +-31 per cent from the mean", and note one observer read four times as much definite hip OA.
4. Minor. pit.observer is one-sided. The paper also reports significant inter-observer correlation in all joints except the wrist, better intra-observer agreement (r to 0.88), and recommends one reader or a two-reader consensus. Hip inter-observer r was only 0.40.
5. Minor. sum.3 locator: the "grade 2+ definite" statement is p.3, not p.1.
6. Minor. pit.scope: use the paper's "local and primary generalized".
7. Minor. Grade 1 wording clash: "minimal" is used for grade 2 in the grade list and "minimal or doubtful" for grade 1 on p.6. The def.g1 hedge is right; consider adding that grade 1 is not counted as definite OA (implied by "Grades 2-4").
8. Minor. The evidence rests on one small sample (85 people aged 55-64, one Lancashire population, 2 observers). Worth one clause in sum.4 so the copilot does not present these correlations as general.
9. Minor. The paper's own list of "eight groups chosen for detailed study" omits PIP, yet Fig 2 shows PIP and the Summary says "eleven joints". Internal inconsistency in the source; the card handles it correctly by following the figures. No action.

## Decision: tier 2 not lookup

Agree. A lookup pack needs a per-grade criterion to apply; grades 1, 3 and 4 have none, and grade 2 is defined only as "definitely present though minimal". Standard films are not data a pack can hold. The card correctly refuses to import the common textbook descriptions.

## For Hassan items

1. DECISION (Atlas needed for per-grade criteria). Agree that the usual descriptions (osteophytes, joint-space narrowing, sclerosis, deformity per grade) are not in this paper and that the Atlas (Kellgren and Lawrence, 1963) is the right source to request. I could not check the Atlas from here. Disagree only with the justification that it "would stay tier 2" because it lacks management: that depends on the library's no-management ruling, which should be stated as such (finding 2). Also ask for the Atlas knee text specifically, since knee and hip carry nearly all real reporting use.
2. Grade 1 wording. Agree with keeping "doubtful" and noting the p.6 wording; this is the faithful treatment.
3. applies_to regions. Disagree in part: the note itself concedes no standard film for lumbar spine or foot, but the card still lists both. Remove foot (finding 1) and consider qualifying lumbar and cervical as apophyseal-joint grading only.
